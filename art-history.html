Art History

Before transitioning into computer science, I trained in art history at UC Berkeley and the Courtauld Institute of Art, where I developed a sustained interest in Baroque visual culture, quadratura, 通景画, and the Jesuit presence at the Chinese imperial court. I welcome conversations and potential collaborations in these areas.

Contact me at wangsq@seas.upenn.edu.

Research Focus

My time at Berkeley gave me a broad foundation in art history across both geography and time, from ancient Greek art to the Pre-Raphaelites. Moving between artists such as Giulio Romano and Wang Hong, I learned to shift my perspective across centuries and cultures, developing an understanding of both Western and East Asian artistic traditions.

At the Courtauld Institute of Art, I developed a more focused interest in transcultural exchange and the movement of artists, ideas, and visual traditions across cultural boundaries. My research focused primarily on Italian Jesuits at the Chinese imperial court during the late eighteenth and early nineteenth centuries.

More broadly, I am deeply interested in transcultural artists across different historical periods, particularly those whose lives and work bridged the Western and East Asian worlds.
